## Description

At 5:58 on the morning of 14 September 2026, a building finishes existing on the north edge of
Wilmington, Delaware. Forty thousand square metres of glass and pale concrete, a crimson six-blade
iris over the doors, four sublevels nobody watched anyone dig. The city's records show it was
approved in 2019. The power company has eleven years of billing for it. Everyone who drives past
is certain it has always been there.

Inside, in an office on the fourth floor, a man opens his eyes behind black glasses and knows
exactly what he is looking at.

He has two lifetimes of memory. One of them ended in a bunker in 1945. The other ended in a volcano,
in a world that never happened here — nobody on this Earth has heard the name Albert Wesker, and
nobody has heard of Umbrella. And he has a System only he can see: a Shop, a Gacha, missions, tiers,
and a rule that makes everything it hands him simply true. A laboratory, a convoy, a private army,
an island — they appear where he decides they appear, and the world remembers them as though they
were always part of it.

He starts with eleven people who would die for him and no idea why. He does not spend the story
asking anyone for permission.

This is the whole ascent, in order, with nothing skipped: the first product, the first Tyrant, the
first company that would not sell, Raccoon City, the security service, the hearing, the stadium, the
treaty, the fleet — and, in the last movement, what happens when something out past the heliopause
finally notices which species is holding the umbrella.

⚠️ What this is: a villain's ascent, told as a catastrophe for everyone around him. The man at its
centre is a monster in both of his lives and the story never pretends otherwise. The real history is
stated plainly and never justified, admired or argued for. Every official, agency, company, scientist
and polity in the story is invented. Nothing here is a manifesto for anything.
