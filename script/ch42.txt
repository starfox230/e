N: They were not looking for us. That is the first thing, and the whole of the next fifty years turns on it.
N: The Tollhouse Compact is a trading association of nineteen species, about four thousand years old, that operates on a principle so simple it took human diplomats a decade to believe it: *a market is cheaper than a war, always, at every scale, without exception.* It has no military. N: In August of 2103 a Compact survey vessel, on a route it had flown for nine hundred years, detected something that had not been there on the previous pass: four hundred and eleven artificial objects in a system that had been catalogued as *pre-contact, industrial, no traffic.*

N: The negotiation lasted four months and the narrator will give you the shape of it, because it is the last time in this account that Albert Wesker is the second most powerful thing in the room and he knows it.
N: The Compact wanted three things: a trade station in the system, most-favoured terms on human biological science — which was, they said without embarrassment, the finest they had encountered in nine hundred years — and an undertaking that humanity would not arm.
N: That last one was not a threat. It was a *sales pitch*. The Compact's entire proposition, four thousand years refined, is that a species which does not arm gets the Compact's protection, and the protection has held nineteen times in four thousand years, and the two occasions it failed are in every Compact schoolroom.
BROKER: (slow) You have four kilometre hulls. You have a drive that we did not sell you and that your physics did not produce. You have an organism in cold storage at your ninth facility that I will not discuss in this room.
BROKER: (slow) We are not asking you to disarm. We are asking you to stop, at the point you have reached, in exchange for four thousand years of accumulated reasons why every species that did not stop is a ruin that we trade over.

WESKER: (very cold) And if we decline?
BROKER: (slow) Then nothing whatsoever happens, Director. That is the part your people keep failing to hear. We do not punish. We simply do not shelter, and in about ninety years the Veth will arrive, and you will discover what that means without us standing between you.
N: He accepted two of the three.
N: The trade station was placed at the fourth Lagrange point in 2104 and is still there. N: He declined the third.
WESKER: (cold) I am not going to promise that my species will not arm, because I am not entitled to promise it. There are eleven billion human beings, four hundred thousand of them in the Free Territory, and about nine hundred of them build ships in orbit, and I have spent forty years making it possible for them to refuse me.
WESKER: (slow) You are asking me to use an authority that I have been trying to dismantle, in order to guarantee something for four hundred years, on behalf of people who are not born.

N: Ashet's reply was eleven words and it is carved, in Compact script and in nine human languages, on the wall of the station at L4.
BROKER: (slow) It is the first answer I have not had to discount.

N: The narrator will spend a page on what the Compact actually did to humanity, because every account concentrates on the Veth and the Choir and the wars, and the thing that changed people's lives was a market.
N: The station opened in 2104 with nineteen species trading on it. By 2107 there were four hundred human businesses on it, of which three hundred and eleven were not Umbrella and eleven were one person with a cargo allowance.
N: What humanity sold: biological science, which the Compact considered the best in nine hundred light years; music, which nobody had expected and which four species developed an expensive habit for; and — this is the part every human schoolchild learns — argument.
BROKER: (slow) You will not understand this and I have stopped trying to explain it politely. Your species produces disagreement of a quality we have not encountered. Not conflict. Conflict is common and cheap. *Formal structured disagreement*, conducted in public, by parties who continue to trade with each other afterwards.
BROKER: (slow) There are eleven species in the Compact who have paid, in hard trade credit, to have human advocates argue their disputes. We have nothing like it. Our disputes are resolved by price.
N: The Compact's archive at L4 contains four thousand years of trade law and, in a wing added in 2111, the complete proceedings of the Human Assembly, translated, updated weekly, consulted by nine species.
N: Umbrella earned nothing from it. It had no part in it. Albert Wesker read the Compact's assessment of it in 2112 and, according to the Red Queen's log, read it four times and then sat for eleven minutes.

N: The narrator will now cover thirty-seven years in a page, because this is a story about one man and because for thirty-seven years the story is not about him.
N: Humanity went out. Not in an exodus and not in an empire: in about four hundred separate attempts, most of them badly funded, nine of which failed with loss of life, and eleven of which became something.

N: The Progenitor lineage went out too.
