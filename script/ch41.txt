[loc] Dock One — 4 June 2091
N: The first ship took seven months to build and it was not built by the System.
N: The narrator wishes to be exact, because it is the point of this chapter and the reason Albert Wesker gave an engineer from the Arcology a pen.
N: The System sold him the hull pattern and the drive geometry: nine billion Points and forty billion Points, delivered as specification — thousands of pages of it, in a form that Crane's people described as *written by somebody who has never had to explain anything to anybody twice*.
N: It did not sell him a ship. He could have bought one. There was a line in the BEYOND tab that would have put a finished four-kilometre vessel in orbit overnight, crewed, with a maintenance log going back nineteen years.
N: He did not buy it. And when Mira Halder asked him why, in the second week, on a gantry, in a pressure suit, he gave her the only fully honest answer of the whole account.
[img] {MIRA} and {WESKER} in pressure suits standing on a gantry inside a vast shipyard, an unfinished hull behind them
WESKER: (cold) Because everything I have ever been given arrived finished, and because I have spent sixty-four years watching what that does to people.
WESKER: (slow) There are four hundred thousand human beings on this planet who cannot refuse an instruction, and there are four hundred thousand more in an ocean who have never had a consequence, and the common cause of both is that I was in a hurry and the catalogue was open.
WESKER: (very cold) So this one gets built. By people. Slowly. With arguments about the budget.
MIRA: (slow) It's going to take seven months and it's going to have a flaw in it.
WESKER: (soft) Yes. And in four hundred years somebody will find the flaw and know that a person put it there, and that will be worth more to them than a ship that came out of a catalogue.

N: They called it the *Christina*, after a river in Delaware that four hundred thousand people in the middle of the Atlantic had never seen.
N: Four kilometres. Crew of eleven hundred. A drive that changed velocity without expelling mass, built to a geometry nobody on earth understood and four people could operate. Nine hundred and forty habitation decks, a hospital, four hydroponic halls — and a garden, on deck eleven, laid out by a woman on an ice shelf who had sent one line in a letter.
N: Mira Halder was chief of structures. She argued with the Directorate about the budget nineteen times and won eleven, which she described afterwards as *the best nineteen arguments of my life*.
N: The narrator will give you one of them, because it is the moment the story changes hands.
[loc] Dock One, construction office — 11 September 2091
MIRA: (slow) I want the redundancy on the drive spars doubled.
WESKER: (cold) The System's specification does not require it.
MIRA: (slow) The System's specification assumes a hull that was placed rather than welded. Mine has four thousand human welds in it and I know exactly how good my welders are, which is very, and not perfect.
WESKER: (soft) It is eleven billion Points and four months.
MIRA: (slow) Then it's eleven billion Points and four months.
[pause 0.8]
WESKER: (very cold) Ms. Halder, are you aware that you are the only person alive who argues with me?
MIRA: (slow) Yes. Everybody's aware. There's a rota — I'm joking. There isn't a rota.
[pause 0.6]
MIRA: (slow) Director, I've thought about this a lot, because people keep telling me I'm brave and I'm not. I argue with you because you have never once, in two years, made me pay for it. Mr. Carrick told me you've been like this since 2033 and that it's the only reason he stayed.
MIRA: (very slow) I think you've spent sixty-five years building an empire that cannot say no to you and hiring, one at a time, the four or five people who can. And I don't think you ever noticed you were doing it.
N: The narrator notes that Albert Wesker approved the doubled redundancy without further discussion, that it cost eleven billion Points and four months, and that in 2134 a spar failure on a sister ship killed nine hundred people and the *Christina* class was the only design in the fleet that survived the same fault.

N: Owen Carrick died on the nineteenth of October 2091, aged ninety-three, at home, having declined Aeterna for the last twenty-two years of his life for reasons he never explained to anyone except his wife, who had taken it and who outlived him by eleven decades.
N: Wesker came on the Thursday. It was the ninth folder, and it was four sentences, and Carrick made him read it out loud in the room.
CARRICK: (very slow) Read it.
WESKER: (soft) *Fifty-eight years. He never once removed a person for telling him the truth, and he never once changed course because of it either.*
WESKER: (soft) *I have concluded that these are the same fact and not two, and that a man who cannot be angered also cannot be reached.*
WESKER: (soft) *I was the mechanism that should have stopped this and I did not work, and I am not sure any mechanism would have, and I am too tired now to know whether that is an excuse.*
WESKER: (soft) *Tell Mira. She is the one who might.*
[pause 1.2]
CARRICK: (very slow) You didn't argue.
WESKER: (very soft) No.
CARRICK: (very slow) Fifty-eight years and that's the first time.
WESKER: (cold) Mr. Carrick, in 2033 you sat in a diner and told me you were taking the job so that you could be the one who told me no. You have done it nine times. I have overruled you eight.
CARRICK: (very slow) Eight?
WESKER: (soft) The ninth was in January 2040 and I wrote *you are correct and I have no answer* at the bottom of it, and I have never told you, and I am telling you now because there is no longer any tactical reason not to and I have discovered that I would like you to know.
[pause 1.0]
N: The narrator records that Owen Carrick laughed, which hurt him, and that he said four more words, and that Albert Wesker sat in that room for another two hours after he had stopped speaking.
CARRICK: (very slow) Fifty-eight years. One.
N: He is buried in Media, Pennsylvania, in an ordinary cemetery, under an ordinary stone. The Umbrella Directorate flew its flag at half mast for the second time in its existence.
N: His nine folders are in a drawer in a desk in a building on the Christina River that appeared out of nothing on the fourteenth of September 2026. They were published, in full, in 2094, on Wesker's instruction, without commentary.
N: They are the primary source for about a third of this account.
