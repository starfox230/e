[loc] The human sphere — 2159
N: The Choir of Ashen Light does not annex. It does not trade, and it does not negotiate, and it has never in eleven thousand years told a lie.
N: It is a theocracy of four species that hears the universe as a single ongoing composition — the physical record calls it a resonance cosmology and the human record has never done better than *they hear it and we do not* — and its entire moral system rests on one proposition: that everything which exists is a note, that notes may be held or released, and that some things are *not notes at all* but noise, and that noise must be ended so that the piece may continue.
N: In eleven thousand years the Choir has declared four species noise. Three are gone. The fourth is the Myriad, and the Choir has been trying since before Earth had an atmosphere with oxygen in it.
N: In 2159 it declared humanity the fifth, and it did so publicly, with an explanation, on every human channel simultaneously, as its doctrine requires.
[img] a radiant robed alien hierarch with many glowing eyes, speaking, pale light, cathedral interior
CHOIR: (slow) You have ended death in your own bodies and you have not asked what death was for. You have made a lineage that does not resolve. You have taken a drive you did not derive and a medicine you did not earn, and you have given both to anyone who asked, without knowing what you were handing out.
CHOIR: (slow) These are not crimes. We do not accuse. A landslide commits no crime.
CHOIR: (very slow) You are a species that has been *given* everything and has therefore learned nothing, and you are propagating at a rate that will place you in nine thousand systems within four hundred years, and there is no note in you. We have listened for fifty-six years. We are very good at listening. There is nothing there.
CHOIR: (slow) You will be ended. It will take approximately ninety years. It will not be cruel. We will begin at your edges and you will have time to understand, because understanding is owed even to noise.
N: The narrator notes that this broadcast was received on four hundred and eleven worlds, that it was accurate in every particular, and that it is the single most damaging thing anyone has ever said about the human species — not because it was hostile, but because eleven billion people read it and a great many of them recognised themselves.

[loc] The Human Assembly — 2159 to 2166
N: The Assembly debated it for seven years.
N: The narrator will not give you the seven years. He will give you the thing that made them unbearable, which is that the Choir was not wrong.
N: Everything humanity had, it had been handed. The drive came out of a catalogue in a panel that one man could see. The medicine came out of a vial that a technician who never existed had labelled *odd* in 2004. The lineage came out of a woman putting a cannula in her own arm at twenty to three in the morning. The cities in the ocean, the plants in the valley, the towers on four skylines, the hundred thousand people in Raccoon City with grandparents in a cemetery — all of it placed, given, delivered finished.
N: And the argument that ran through four hundred worlds for seven years was not *how do we fight them*. It was: *are they right?*
N: Albert Wesker was one hundred and thirty-three years old. He gave evidence to the Assembly once, in 2161, for four hours, and refused to be called again.
[img] {WESKER} standing alone on a debating floor, delegates tiered above him, cold formal light
WESKER: (slow) They are right about me.
WESKER: (cold) Everything the Choir described is an accurate description of Albert Wesker. I was given a catalogue at the age of thirty-eight in an empty office and I have spent a hundred and thirty-three years spending it, and I did not derive one single thing I have handed out, and I have never once been able to answer the question of what the counterparty wanted.
[pause 0.8]
WESKER: (very cold) They are wrong about you, and I am going to tell you why, and then I am going to sit down and this Assembly is going to decide without me, as it has decided everything since 2119.
WESKER: (slow) The Choir listened to this species for fifty-six years and found no note in it. I have listened to it for a hundred and thirty-three and I can tell you exactly where they were looking, because I made the same error for the first sixty.
WESKER: (cold) They listened to what we *have*. Of course there is nothing there; it was all handed to us, by me, out of a box. If a species is the sum of its possessions then this one is a warehouse.
WESKER: (very slow) They did not listen to the argument.
[pause 1.0]
WESKER: (slow) For forty years this species has done nothing but argue. It argued about whether it was entitled to the medicine it was already taking. It argued for seven years about whether to submit to the Veth and answered them in four hundred different voices and won. There is an assembly on this platform that has ruled against my organisation nineteen times and been obeyed nineteen times. There is a woman on Ochoa who built a colony and then resigned because she thought the builder should never govern it.
WESKER: (cold) None of that was given to anybody. Every part of it was made, slowly, badly, by people who had every material reason not to bother, against the wishes of the most powerful individual who has ever lived — and I want this Assembly to understand that I mean that literally, because I fought most of it and lost.
[pause 0.6]
WESKER: (very cold) That is the note. It is not in our possessions. It is in the fact that we are the only species in nine hundred light years that has spent forty years shouting at the thing that fed it.
N: The Assembly's answer to the Choir was drafted by a committee of nineteen, went through four hundred amendments, and was transmitted in 2166. It is eleven words long and it was written by a schoolteacher from Raccoon City who was two hundred and nine years old.
WOMAN: (slow) *Come and listen again. We have been busy since you started.*

[loc] The edge of the human sphere — 2167 to 2181
N: The Choir came anyway. Doctrine is doctrine.
N: The narrator will describe the war of the edges in one page, because it is the only shooting war in this account and because Albert Wesker did not command it.
N: It was fought by the Assembly's defence council, under a doctrine written in 2144 by a committee, and executed by admirals who had been colonial engineers. It lasted fourteen years. It was fought at the edges, as the Choir had promised, and the Choir kept every single one of its undertakings, because it has never in eleven thousand years told a lie.
N: Human losses: four hundred and eleven thousand dead at nine settlements, all of them evacuated too slowly, none of them targeted after evacuation was complete.
N: The Choir stopped four times in fourteen years, unilaterally, each time for about nine months, each time while it *listened again* — which is in its doctrine, which no other species had ever survived long enough to trigger, and which it had not performed in eleven thousand years.
N: And in 2181, at a system with no name at the edge of the sphere, a Choir hierarch stood in a human assembly hall that had been built out of a cargo module, in front of four hundred delegates from nine worlds who had been arguing about evacuation quotas for six days, and listened to them do it for eleven hours.
[img] a radiant robed alien standing silently at the back of a crowded makeshift human assembly hall
[pause 1.0]
CHOIR: (very slow) We were mistaken.
CHOIR: (slow) There is a note. It is not where any of our doctrine instructed us to listen, and it is not in anything you hold, and it is not in any of your works. It is in the disagreement, which does not resolve, and which we had classified as disorder for eleven thousand years.
CHOIR: (very slow) We have ended three species. We will now go and be quiet for a very long time, and consider what we have done, and we ask nothing of you, because we are not entitled to ask.
N: The Choir of Ashen Light withdrew from the human sphere in 2181 and has not been heard from since. Its last transmission was the eleven-word answer of 2166, repeated once, which the Compact's xenologists believe was an apology and which nobody has ever been able to confirm.
