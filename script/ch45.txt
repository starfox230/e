N: The Myriad has no name for itself, no government, no doctrine, no art and no history, and it has been at war with everything it has ever met for somewhere between four hundred thousand and nine hundred thousand years.
N: It is a hive. That word is wrong in nine ways and the human record has never found a better one. It is not a species with a queen; it is a single distributed organism of somewhere over four hundred trillion bodies, occupying eleven hundred systems, that grows into any volume it is not prevented from entering, and that has never once in its recorded existence responded to a signal.
N: The Compact has traded with everything in nine hundred light years except the Myriad. The Veth have fought it for ninety thousand years and hold their border with four hundred thousand ships. The Choir declared it noise before the Earth had oxygen and has not been able to end it.
N: It reached the human sphere in 2194, at a mining system called the Kelpie Reach, with eleven thousand bodies, and ate the system in nine days.

N: Human dead: four thousand one hundred. There was no evacuation because there was no warning and no demand and no negotiation, because there is nobody there to negotiate with.
N: And the narrator will tell you plainly what that did to a species that had spent forty years learning to argue: it broke the argument.
N: Within four months the Human Assembly had voted, by three hundred and eighty-one to thirty, to place the defence of the sphere under a single unified command with emergency powers — the exact instrument it had spent seventy years refusing to create — and to offer that command to the one person alive who had fought anything.

N: He refused.
SENATOR: (slow) Director, four thousand one hundred people —
WESKER: (very cold) I am aware of the number. I have read the names, as I read the four hundred and eleven thousand names of the Choir war, every morning, for fourteen years.
WESKER: (slow) I am refusing because this Assembly is about to hand a hundred and sixty-eight years of work back to the man it took it from, in four months, out of fear, and because I have spent seventy years of my life making it impossible for one person to hold this species and I am not going to undo it in an afternoon because something frightening has arrived.
SENATOR: (slow) And if the Myriad takes another system?
WESKER: (very cold) Then it takes another system, and you will have to fight it as what you are, which is eleven billion people and four hundred worlds and an argument. I will fight in it. I will take any command this Assembly gives me that has a term limit, a reporting line and a review. I will not take emergency powers, and if you vote them to me I will decline them in public, on the record, and that will be worse for you than if you had never offered.
[pause 1.0]
N: The narrator notes that the Assembly voted the powers anyway, that he declined them in public on the record, and that the resulting constitutional crisis lasted eleven months while the Myriad ate two more systems.
N: Nine thousand more people died in those eleven months. The Assembly's own inquiry in 2203 concluded that a unified command in 2194 would have saved most of them, and that Albert Wesker's refusal was the correct decision, and the narrator has read all four hundred pages of that inquiry and has never been able to decide whether the two findings can both be true.

N: Human losses: nineteen thousand.
N: Myriad losses: unknowable, because it does not have units and does not retreat, and because the word *loss* assumes something that can be lost.

N: On the fourth of November 2210 an eleven-person boarding team took the interior of a Myriad mass for the first time, and four of them came back, and Albert Wesker carried two of them.
N: What they brought back was the thing that ended the war, and it is not what anybody expected, and the narrator will describe it as carefully as the record allows.
N: They found no command structure. They found no individuals. What they found, in a chamber four kilometres inside a body of four hundred trillion, was a *memory*: a structure of accreted mineral and tissue, nine hundred thousand years old, in which the entire history of everything the Myriad had ever eaten was recorded, in order, in detail, with nothing missing.
N: Eleven hundred species. Four hundred trillion bodies' worth of experience. Nine hundred thousand years.
N: It is the largest archive in the known volume of space, and it belongs to a thing that has never once used it for anything, because there is nobody in there to read it.
[pause 1.0]
N: Dr. Evelyn Marsh spent eleven years on it.
N: Her conclusion, published in 2221 and never seriously challenged, is four sentences, and it is the most disturbing document in this account.
EVELYN: (slow) The Myriad is not hostile. It is not anything. It is a growth process with a perfect memory and no capacity whatsoever to act on it.
EVELYN: (slow) Every species it has consumed is preserved in it, in full, and none of them are alive, and the structure has no mechanism for retrieval.
EVELYN: (slow) It has been carrying eleven hundred civilisations for nine hundred thousand years the way a glacier carries a rock.
EVELYN: (very slow) I have spent my life making things that do not die. This is what that looks like when nobody is home.
