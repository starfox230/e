[loc] The Ochoa system — 4 April 2141
N: The Veth Hegemony is older than the oceans of Earth. It has four hundred thousand worlds, a caste structure that has not changed in nine hundred thousand years, and a founding doctrine that the human record translates, imperfectly, as *the settled question.*
N: The settled question is this: every species that reaches the void either submits to an order or imposes one, and the Veth reached the void first, and therefore the order exists, and therefore submission is not a defeat but an administrative formality that has already taken place and merely needs to be acknowledged.
N: They have annexed eleven hundred species. They have fought four wars in ninety thousand years. The wars are short.
N: They arrived at Ochoa — a human colony of nine hundred thousand people, forty-three years old, with a dock, two ships and a hydroponic economy — on the fourth of April 2141, with thirteen vessels, and they did not fire.
[img] a colonial administrator and officials facing a huge holographic alien figure in a colony council chamber
VETH: (slow) You are informed. The system is within the settled order. Your administration continues. Your production is assessed at one part in nine. Your vessels will be registered. A garrison will arrive in four hundred days.
N: The colony's administrator — a woman of sixty-one named Theresa Okonkwo-Vance, who had been elected eight months earlier on a platform about water rights — asked the question that every annexed species has asked and that the Veth have a standard answer for.
WOMAN: (slow) And if we decline?
VETH: (slow) Then the system is within the settled order, your administration does not continue, and a garrison arrives in four hundred days.
N: She transmitted the exchange to Earth and to every human world, in the clear, unencrypted, within the hour, which was not in any protocol and which is the reason the next fifty years went the way they did.
N: Nine hundred and eleven human settlements received it inside four months. By the time it reached Earth the argument had already been running for a third of a year on four hundred worlds, in public, and Albert Wesker had not said a word.

N: The narrator wants to describe the body that decided this, because it did not exist in 2091 and because building it is the only thing Albert Wesker ever did that he could not do quickly.
N: The Human Assembly had four hundred and eleven seats, elected from the worlds by population, meeting on Earth and by relay. It had existed for nineteen years. It controlled the shared defence budget, the Compact treaties, the drive registry and the Progenitor protocols, and it had no executive at all: the Directorate implemented what it voted, and the Hangzhou tribunal ruled on whether the Directorate had.
N: Umbrella held no seats. Albert Wesker had a chair on the floor, not on the benches, with the status of a witness who may be called and may not vote, and he had proposed that arrangement himself in 2119 and the Assembly had spent four years arguing about whether it was too generous or an insult before adopting it unchanged.
N: On the nineteenth of September 2141 the Assembly called him.

SENATOR: (slow) Director, the Assembly has four proposals before it. Submit. Refuse and negotiate. Refuse and fight. Evacuate Ochoa.
SENATOR: (slow) You are the only person alive who has fought anything. You are also, by your own repeated statement, not entitled to decide this. So I am going to ask you the only question the Assembly can properly ask a witness. What is true?

WESKER: (slow) Four things.
WESKER: (cold) One. If we fight them with what we have, we lose. Not narrowly. I have had the Red Queen on it since April and the model does not have an outcome in which a human fleet engages a Veth battle group and anything human survives the engagement. Anyone who tells this Assembly otherwise is selling you something.
WESKER: (slow) Two. If we submit, we survive, and the terms are genuinely one part in nine, and the Veth have honoured that assessment for ninety thousand years, and eleven hundred species live under it and most of them are not unhappy. I want that on the record honestly. Submission is not extinction. It is a tax and a garrison and a ceiling.
WESKER: (very cold) Three. The ceiling is the point. Under the settled order no species may hold a drive it did not receive from the Hegemony, may not exceed an assessed production, and may not — this is the clause that matters — alter its own biology without licence. Aeterna is unlicensed. The Progenitor lineage is unlicensed. There are four hundred thousand human beings alive today who would be, under Veth law, a category the translation renders as *unpermitted persons*.

SENATOR: (slow) And the fourth thing?
WESKER: (very soft) The fourth thing is that they have made a mistake, and I do not think anybody in this chamber has seen it yet, and I have been looking at it for five months.

WESKER: (cold) The Veth have annexed eleven hundred species. In every case they have approached a species' *authority* — a throne, a council, a hierarch — and informed it, and the authority has either acknowledged or been removed and replaced with one that would.
WESKER: (slow) Their protocol has no procedure for a species that cannot be informed, because it does not have a single point that speaks for it.
WESKER: (very cold) There are four hundred and eleven worlds, eighteen billion people, nine hundred settlements, four legal systems and this Assembly, which cannot bind a colony that votes against it. If every one of those bodies answers the Veth separately, at its own pace, in its own words, with different answers, the Hegemony is confronted with an administrative condition it has not encountered in ninety thousand years.
SENATOR: (slow) You are proposing that we defeat an interstellar empire with *paperwork*.
[pause 0.6]
WESKER: (soft) Senator, I built a company that owned half this planet's infrastructure and no government ever laid a finger on it, and I did it with nothing else.

N: The narrator will give you one of the four hundred separate answers, because the campaign is usually described from the Assembly and it did not happen there.
N: Ochoa acknowledged the settled order in 2142, unanimously, and Theresa Okonkwo-Vance — who had transmitted the Veth's demand in the clear and started all of it — voted for acknowledgement, which nine hundred thousand people found difficult to understand.
WOMAN: (slow) I'll say it plainly because the recording goes out at six. We are acknowledging. We are not submitting; there is a difference and it is the whole of our position.
WOMAN: (slow) Their protocol requires us to acknowledge that the system is within the settled order. We do acknowledge that. It is a statement about where their maps say we are. It is not a statement about who we take instruction from, and I have read all four hundred pages of their annexation law twice and it does not say that it is.
WOMAN: (very slow) And then we are going to file four hundred and eleven procedural queries about the assessment, which their own law requires them to answer individually, in writing, before a garrison may be seated.
COUNCILLOR: (slow) How long does that take them?
WOMAN: (slow) Their fastest recorded response to a single query is nine days. We have four hundred and eleven. And every colony that files behind us adds its own.

N: The narrator notes that Theresa Okonkwo-Vance had been a fisheries lawyer.

N: It took seven years and it is the most-studied campaign in human history, and not one human being died in it.
N: Ochoa acknowledged the settled order in 2142, unanimously, and then filed four hundred and eleven procedural queries about the assessment, which the Veth protocol requires be answered individually before a garrison may be seated.
N: The Free Territory refused outright and invited a garrison, which arrived and found a platform city of four hundred thousand that had voted, nine days earlier, to dissolve its own administration, so that there was nothing for the garrison to be seated over. It sat there for eleven years.
N: Sixty-one colonies acknowledged. Forty refused. Nineteen acknowledged and then seceded from the Assembly, which under Veth law made their acknowledgement void and required a fresh approach.
N: The Compact — which has no military and has never held territory and which had spent thirty-eight years watching humanity with an interest that its own historians called *unseemly* — filed nine hundred trade objections, every one of them valid under a treaty the Veth had signed in the human year 1611.
N: And the Veth admiral, whose name renders as Sethra-Ka and who had annexed forty species in her career without ever raising her voice, requested a summit.

VETH: (slow) You are the cause of this.
WESKER: (cold) No. I proposed it. Eighteen billion people decided to do it, in about four hundred separate votes, over seven years, and about a third of them did it wrong and two colonies did it in a way I strongly advised against.
VETH: (slow) You are the authority.
WESKER: (very cold) Admiral, that is the assumption your entire order rests on, and it is the only thing you have got wrong. I have been trying to stop being the authority since I was a hundred and eleven years old. It has taken me seventy years and it is not finished and it is the hardest thing I have ever done.
[pause 1.0]
VETH: (slow) Why.
N: The narrator records that the question was one word, that the translation software rendered it without punctuation, and that Albert Wesker took a long time over the answer.
WESKER: (slow) Because I was given a catalogue and no oversight when I was young, in another world, and I built something magnificent out of it, and then I did it again here, and both times the thing I built was perfect and nobody in it could say no to me.
WESKER: (cold) The first one ended in a volcano. This one is going to end in an argument that will run for nine hundred years and that I will not win, and I have come to prefer the second outcome, and it took me about sixty years to arrive at that and I am not certain I would arrive there again.

VETH: (slow) The settled order has held for ninety thousand years.
WESKER: (very cold) Yes. And in ninety thousand years it has produced eleven hundred species that have never once surprised you, and one afternoon in a system of no importance you were surprised by a hydroponic colony with two ships. I would ask you to consider what that is worth, because it is the only thing in this room that neither of us can manufacture.
N: The Hegemony withdrew from the Ochoa system in 2151 under an instrument it insisted on calling a *deferral of assessment*, and which every species in nine hundred light years read correctly.
N: It is the only recorded case of a Veth withdrawal without combat in ninety thousand years, and the Veth have never permitted it to be called anything else, and the Compact has a word for it that the human record renders as *the long polite no*.
