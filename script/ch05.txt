N: There are two kinds of people in an organisation, and Albert Wesker had spent four months working with only one of them.

[pause 0.5]

N: The narrator would like to record that this conversation lasted ninety seconds, that Albert Wesker had prepared for it for six hours, and that he had not said one single word that was untrue.
N: That is the whole trick. He never needed to lie to a clever person. He needed only to be the first human being in their life who had read them properly.

WESKER: Whatever you ask for. It is the least interesting question you could have asked and I will forgive you for it once.
N: She came on the first of February. She gave four weeks' notice the next morning and spent the last of them barely sleeping.

N: Catherine Adeyemi was thirty-eight, British, twenty-seven years in London private equity and two in a fund that had recently taken an interest in American diagnostics. N: They were, in fact, impossible. That was the trouble with everything the System did. The paperwork was perfect, the history was perfect, the witnesses were perfect — and the *rates* were wrong. A company does not go from nothing to four hundred and six placed instruments in ninety days. A plant does not appear in a filing with fourteen years of depreciation and no capital expenditure to match. Every individual document was flawless. The shape they made together was a lie, and Catherine Adeyemi read shapes for a living.
