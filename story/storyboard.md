# Storyboard — 50 chapters, 6 Parts

Target ~110,000 words of script ≈ 11h45m of narration plus titles and music breaths. Each chapter ~2,200 words ≈ 14 minutes.

**Rules for every chapter:** Wesker is the subject. Something of his grows — a facility, a roster, a capability, a territory, a rank. There is one showpiece (◆) the viewer will remember. Obstacles are resolved inside the chapter. The System does something (□). The world reacts in a line or two (✦). Adolf appears only where performance matters (▲), and the narrator always cues him.

---

## PART I — THE GARAGE EMPIRE (Sep – Dec 2026) · Tier I

### 1. The Building That Was Always There
- Cold open in the dark: a voice with no body running a selection. Two candidate wills, two recorded deaths — a bunker in 1945, a volcano in 2009 — named in a few sentences and left behind. *Merge approved. Host: Wesker, Albert. Passenger: secondary will, non-dominant.*
- ◆ 14 September 2026, 5:58 a.m., Wilmington, Delaware. A six-storey white stone building with a crimson iris over the door finishes existing on a riverfront lot where there had been a parking apron. A jogger runs past it without breaking stride. A traffic camera has no record of the change and the city's property system has held the deed since 1998.
- 6:00 a.m. Wesker opens his eyes in a leather chair behind a desk on the sixth floor, in his own coat, his own glasses folded on the blotter. He knows the body. He knows the taste of the virus in it. He remembers dying and he remembers who did it. He is not confused for one second; he is *interested*.
- □ The System opens. Full tour, panel by panel: STATUS, POINTS, SHOP (seven tabs), GACHA and its rarities, MISSIONS, ROSTER, and the placement rule — *what you purchase appears where you choose; the world will account for it.* Tier I is labelled **GARAGE**. He asks, flatly, how one insults a man in a language with no adjectives.
- The elevator down. His eleven, waiting as if they have worked here for years: Hale and two security officers; Gus and one technician; Pell; Dr. Lena Voss and three junior scientists; Marguerite at the desk. ROSTER: *Loyalty — Absolute.* He tests it once, quietly, with an order no employee would obey, and it is obeyed.
- The tour of his own empty house: five floors of finished, humming, staffed-for-nobody laboratory, and a three-level garage with nothing parked in it.
- ▲ The other voice speaks for the first time, cued by the narrator: it wants to know where the crowd is. Wesker tells it there is no crowd yet, and that it will be given the microphone when a microphone is worth having.
- □ **MAIN MISSION: TIER II — COMPANY.** Requirements: a saleable product, $50M in assets, fifty staff. Reward: 25,000 Points, Territory tab, the Company banner.
- ✦ Across the street, a coffee shop owner tells a customer the white building's people are finally moving back in after the renovation. She has no memory of a renovation. She does not look for one.
- Close: Wesker at the window at dusk over the Christina River, putting the glasses on. He has done this before, with other men's money, under other men's names, and it took him thirty years and ended in fire. He intends to do it in three.

### 2. Sublevel B4
- Morning. He reads everything the building contains: freezer archive, sequencers, thirty years of someone's cell lines that have never existed anywhere but here.
- □ First Shop purchase, 6,000 of his 10,000 starting Points: **Laboratory Sublevel B4** — a BSL-4 level with containment, a cold vault and a freight elevator. He places it beneath the garage's lowest floor.
- ◆ It arrives at 3 a.m.: no noise, no dust. Gus reports in the morning that the elevator now has a fourth button, that it has always had a fourth button, and that he has the keys for it on the ring he has carried for six years. Wesker watches him say it and understands exactly what he owns.
- Science: he works B4 himself through the night and shows what he is — reconstructing a stalled viral-vector line from the archive by spotting that the previous team's "failed" transfections were fine and their *normalization* was wrong. Voss watches a man solve in one night what her notes say took a team two years.
- □ First earned Points: *acting brilliantly* (+900). Stat points into Intelligence.
- He buys two more summoned lab technicians (Personnel) and places them as new hires; HR's records already show their interviews.
- ▲ The voice wants him to tell his people what they are part of. Not yet, he says: people who are told the plan negotiate; people who are shown a miracle obey.
- ✦ A Delaware business wire lists a routine filing: Umbrella Research Division Seven, Wilmington. Nobody reads it.
- Close: the cold vault door closing on the first thing he has made in this world with his own hands.

### 3. A Product Worth Owning
- □ Shop, Blueprints: **Rapid Pathogen Assay** (2,500). A cheap, fast, reliable diagnostic panel — legal, clean, boring, and something every hospital on Earth wants.
- The System delivers it as a complete package: process, validation data, and a plant-ready manufacturing spec. Wesker improves it in an afternoon, because he can, and the improvement is his name on the patent.
- Pell's first miracle of paperwork: regulatory clearance, distribution, and a price that undercuts the market by forty percent.
- Obstacle, one chapter long: **Graham Tolliver**, CEO of Verdant Axis Therapeutics, whose company sells the assay Umbrella just made obsolete. He comes to Wilmington to buy the patent, and brings a lawyer and a threat.
- ◆ The meeting. Wesker arrives last, says almost nothing, lets Tolliver's own lawyer explain why Verdant Axis cannot survive a lawsuit it would win. He offers Tolliver a licence at a price that is an insult and watches him take it.
- □ +14,000 Points (revenue, and a rival made to pay). Level up. Shop prices unchanged; his budget is not.
- ✦ A hospital network in Philadelphia switches suppliers. Then two more. A trade paper calls Umbrella "the quiet Delaware entrant".
- Close: Pell reads the first month's numbers aloud. Wesker asks him to read them again, slower, because he wants to hear what a foundation sounds like.

### 4. Rare
- Points enough for the Gacha. The System explains the banner and the rarity ladder; a ten-pull guarantees a Rare.
- □ Ten-pull. Commons and Uncommons: three summoned technicians, a fleet of black vehicles, a security blueprint, a stat token. Uncommon: **Surveillance Suite**. Rare, in blue light: **Tyrant, Series T-103, in a cryogenic cradle.**
- ◆ The cradle is placed in B4. Frost on glass, three metres of engineered soldier breathing once a minute. Wesker stands with his hand flat on the glass and finds, to his mild surprise, that he is enjoying himself. The eyes open. It knows him.
- ▲ The voice, quietly, wants to know how many. Wesker: eventually, as many as he likes. Today, one, and nobody will ever be told about it.
- □ Second Shop purchase: **Newark Plant** — a pharmaceutical production plant twelve miles up the interstate. He places it on a vacant industrial lot.
- ◆ The plant appears overnight with three hundred employees, a union local, a twenty-year safety record and a sign that has faded in the sun. A local reporter drives past it twice and only notices on the second pass, then decides he had always noticed.
- Assay production moves in-house. Margins triple.
- ✦ Vignette: the plant's night-shift supervisor, on the phone to his wife, says work is the same as ever. He has been employed there, as far as he remembers, for eleven years. He is four days old.
- Close: the System's roster now reads twenty-two Absolute, three hundred and eleven integrated. Wesker asks what the difference is. *Integrated personnel can quit.* He says he will keep that in mind.

### 5. Two Humans
- He wants what summoned staff cannot give: ambition, invention, doubt turned into loyalty. So he recruits, and he does it by being better than anyone they have ever met.
- **Dr. Evelyn Marsh**, 27, a postdoc at Penn whose brilliant paper nobody read. He reads it. In her own lab, in ninety seconds, he tells her what is wrong with her negative controls and what her data actually proves — which is bigger than what she claimed. She follows him to the car.
- **Catherine Adeyemi**, 38, London private equity, flies to Wilmington to short a company whose numbers are impossible. She comes to expose him. He shows her the plant, the assay, the order book, and offers her the CFO's chair with one condition: she may argue with him about money and about nothing else.
- □ ROSTER changes shape: humans arrive with a Loyalty percentage. Evelyn 61%. Catherine 22%. *Human loyalty is earned. Consider how.*
- ▲ The voice calls it a weakness to employ anyone who can leave. Wesker calls it the difference between an army and a machine, and reminds it who ended up in the bunker.
- ◆ Evelyn's first day in B4: she is cleared to the fourth floor, not the fourth button, and still walks out with the look of a woman who has seen the future.
- □ Level 5. **Command Presence I** (Abilities, 5,000): rooms quiet when he speaks. His Presence is still his worst stat and he knows it.
- ✦ Verdant Axis's board minutes note "unusual competitive pressure from a private Delaware firm".
- Close: the two women at the elevator, comparing first impressions. Catherine: "He is the most dangerous person I have ever been paid by." Evelyn: "Yes." She says it like a prayer.

### 6. The Campus
- Umbrella needs room. He does not buy land; he buys **buildings**, and the land under them adjusts.
- □ Shop, Facilities: four riverfront buildings — Research II, Logistics, the Data Hall, and a five-hundred-seat auditorium — 38,000 Points.
- ◆ The riverfront changes between Friday night and Saturday morning. Five buildings, a walled service road, a helipad, and a crimson iris on every door. The Christina River waterfront district's website has photographs of the campus from 2004.
- ✦ World texture: a city planner finds the permits in her own handwriting; a drone hobbyist's old footage now shows the buildings; a conspiracy forum notices and is laughed at. Two hundred new jobs; the mayor's office issues a delighted statement.
- ▲ First real use of the other voice: Wesker must address four hundred staff in the new auditorium, and he is cold in front of crowds. Adolf teaches him stagecraft in the car — where to stand, how long to wait, that a crowd must be made to lean forward before it is given anything. Wesker executes it with his own ice.
- ◆ The speech. Four minutes, no notes. He promises them no comfort and no security; he promises them that the work they do here will outlive every government now standing. They stand up.
- □ +Points: devotion. Presence 16 → 21. *Note: the passenger's craft is operationally useful.* Wesker files that away with some irritation.
- Close: Catherine, on the roof, asking where the money for five buildings came from. Wesker tells her the truth in a sentence she cannot use, and she understands she has just been made complicit.

### 7. The Man Who Would Not Sell
- Tolliver stops negotiating. Verdant Axis sues over the archive licence, poaches at Umbrella's doors, and hires a private intelligence firm to find out what is in the Delaware basement.
- Wesker does not defend. He attacks with paper first: Pell and Catherine buy Verdant Axis's debt from a bank that is happy to be rid of it.
- ◆ Night at the campus: four contractors come over the service wall with cameras. Hale's officers are already standing where they intend to land. They see one thing they cannot explain — a man crossing forty metres of open ground in the time it takes a camera shutter to close — and they walk out unharmed and unable to give their employer a coherent report.
- The board meeting in Trenton. Wesker arrives last, and this time he takes off the glasses. Tolliver resigns in the room, and the story of what he saw across the table gets him quietly retired from the industry.
- □ **Corporate conquest: Verdant Axis Therapeutics acquired.** +40,000 Points. Assets pass $200M.
- ✦ The trade press calls it the fastest hostile acquisition in the sector's history. Two analysts argue about the financing on television.
- Close: the name on the Trenton building comes down. Wesker has the new one installed the same night: **Umbrella Life Sciences, Inc.**

### 8. Arklay
- All requirements met. □ **TIER II — COMPANY.** 25,000 Points, the Territory tab, and the Company banner unlocks.
- The banner's rates are explained: Legendary is not a single item but a **package**. He pulls ten.
- □ Epic: **Anti-Serum Blueprint Line**. Rare: a second Tyrant. Then gold light floods an office with nobody in it but him, and the System takes its time: **LEGENDARY — THE ARKLAY ESTATE.** A mansion in the Allegheny forest and a laboratory complex of nine levels beneath it, complete with its own power plant, security detachment, forty-one research staff — and two names he knows: **Dr. William Birkin** and **Dr. Annette Birkin.**
- ◆ He flies out at dawn. The house he remembers from another life, in the wrong forest, in the right decade, with his own iris on the gate. Birkin comes down the steps talking too fast about an idea he had on the way here, exactly as he always did, and calls him Albert.
- Wesker, for three seconds, is not in control of his own face. Then he is.
- ▲ The voice observes that he has just been given back his dead. Wesker: they are not back. They are *issued*. And unlike last time, they cannot betray him.
- The nine levels: containment wings, a cryo hall, and one sealed vault the System will not price. *PROGENITOR — not yet.*
- ✦ In the valley below, a state road crew notices a new private access road and marks it on the map as existing.
- Close of Part I: Wesker on the mansion's terrace above the fog with Birkin talking behind him, saying the only sentimental thing he will say in fifty years: "This time, no Spencer. No board. No one above me." The System's title line changes: *Umbrella — Tier II. Director: Albert Wesker.*

## PART II — THE COMPANY THAT SHOULD NOT EXIST (2027 – 2028) · Tier II

### 9. Aeterna
- Birkin and Evelyn on the same bench for the first time; Wesker steers both.
- □ Blueprint: **Cellular Renewal Compound**, sold as *Aeterna* — a genuine, demonstrable, legal reversal of a decade of cellular ageing markers.
- ◆ The data release. A 68-year-old trial volunteer's immune profile reads like a 40-year-old's on a screen in front of a thousand journalists. Umbrella's valuation goes from $2B to $61B in nine trading days.
- Wesker's science moment: he explains the mechanism (and its deliberately withheld limit) so clearly that a hostile panel of immunologists applauds a man they came to destroy.
- ✦ Vignettes: a waiting list in Singapore; a bill in Congress; a cardinal's sermon; a pharmacist in Ohio who cannot get stock.
- □ Points flood. Level 20. He puts everything into Intelligence and Viral Affinity.
- Close: Catherine tells him he is now richer than the company that made him. Loyalty 54%.

### 10. Raccoon City
- □ Territory tab, first purchase: **a company city.** Population 100,000, in the Allegheny valley beneath Arklay. Placed.
- ◆ It appears over one night in October: a downtown, a hospital, a police department, a tram line, schools, a football stadium, and a hundred thousand people who have always lived there, with birth certificates and childhood photographs and a high school reunion next spring. Wesker drives in at dawn through his own suburbs and the traffic lights change for him.
- The horror is in the details: the Mayor is summoned (Absolute) and the Chief of Police is not; a woman on a porch tells a reporter she has lived in Raccoon City forty years and cannot name the town she grew up in, and laughs it off.
- ✦ The census bureau records an "administrative correction". A national outlet runs a puff piece about the miracle of company towns. Somebody's uncle on a forum screams the truth into a void.
- □ +250,000 Points. Umbrella now has citizens. *Devotion income: continuous.*
- Close: standing on the stadium's top row looking at the lights of a city he bought, the other voice asks whether they can be made to chant. Not yet, says Wesker. Not for years. But yes.

### 11. The Umbrella Security Service
- A rival's stolen sample, a hijacked shipment: Umbrella needs its own force.
- □ Epic pull: **HUNK**, and with him the doctrine, kit and training pipeline of the **U.S.S.** Hale commands; HUNK operates.
- ◆ First operation: a clean, silent, twenty-two-minute recovery from a rival research site, filmed by nobody. The narrator walks it beat by beat.
- Wesker watches the whole thing from B4 on Surveillance Suite and says four words the entire time.
- □ Military-adjacent Shop items unlock early.
- Close: a Tyrant walks a dark corridor in Arklay, and HUNK, who fears nothing, steps aside.

### 12. Towers
- Global expansion by purchase, not construction.
- ◆ □ Four **Umbrella Towers** — London, Singapore, Zurich, Tokyo — appear in one weekend, each one a plausible ten-year-old landmark on a skyline photographed a million times. Skyline stock photographs update; tourists' old holiday pictures now have a tower in them.
- Regulators in three countries open files and find, in every case, a decade of immaculate compliance.
- ✦ A Zurich banker realizes his own bank has financed the tower he can see from his office and cannot find the file. He stops looking, because the loan is performing.
- □ Tier III requirements posted: sovereign-level contracts, a second continent, 100,000 staff.
- Close: four cities, four crimson irises, one man on a plane.

### 13. The Keynote
- The industry's biggest stage, in Basel. Umbrella is invited to be looked at.
- ▲ Adolf's chapter. The craft is laid out in full — arrival, silence, the first sentence, the pause that makes a room hold its breath — as the passenger drills the host in a hotel suite, and the narrator makes clear that the words are Wesker's and only the timing is not.
- ◆ Twenty-two minutes on a dark stage with no slides. He offers the world biosecurity, longevity, and the end of pandemics, and asks for nothing. Four thousand people stand. The video passes 200 million views in a week.
- □ Presence 21 → 34. *Devotion: global, diffuse, measurable.* +900,000 Points.
- ✦ Vignettes from the world: a teenager in Manila with an Umbrella phone wallpaper; a French minister's staff drafting an approach; a virologist in Atlanta who watches it three times and cannot say why she is frightened.
- Close: the other voice, almost gentle: *now* they will chant. Wesker: now they will *sign*.

### 14. What the Camera Saw
- A competitor's security camera catches him crossing a loading bay at a speed no human body permits.
- Rather than bury it, Wesker releases it himself through three channels as a marketing stunt, and the truth becomes unbelievable by being everywhere.
- ◆ The set-piece: he repeats it, on purpose, on a live stage, as a "demonstration of our motion-capture partner". A hundred million people watch the real thing and call it a fake.
- □ Hidden mission complete: *Hide in the light.* Reward: **Ability — Mundane Immunity** (disease, toxins, and every ordinary poison).
- Close: Evelyn, who has done the maths on the footage and is the first human being to say out loud what he is. He tells her the truth. She asks to be next. Loyalty 96%.

### 15. The Inquiry
- Three federal agencies and a Senate staffer begin asking how a company gets this big this fast.
- Wesker does not obstruct. He **invites** them: full access to four sites he chose, a hundred thousand pages, and the most helpful general counsel in America.
- ◆ The showpiece is his handling of the lead investigator: a woman who arrives certain and leaves with a career-making report that is entirely true and entirely wrong.
- □ Intelligence tab unlocks (Shop): dossiers, listening, influence.
- ✦ The report is published. Umbrella's stock rises on it.
- Close: Sloane's name appears for the first time, in a dossier Wesker reads with real interest. *A professional. Finally.*

### 16. Tier III
- Sovereign contracts: the United States, then the EU, buy the national biosecurity stockpile from Umbrella. Africa and Asia get the assay at cost — a gift that buys two continents' goodwill and an enormous Points payout.
- □ **TIER III — AGENCY.** The Progenitor rarity appears in the banner for the first time.
- ◆ Twenty-pull on the Agency banner. Legendary package: **The Antarctic Facility**, staff, a Tyrant cohort of eight, and a research fleet. Progenitor (black with a crimson edge): a sealed ampoule the System labels *ORIGIN — do not open until Affinity 40.*
- ▲ The voice wants to know what happens at forty. Wesker says he will tell him when he is thirty-nine.
- Close of Part II: the Arklay vault door, and behind it the sealed thing, waiting. Umbrella has 140,000 employees and no rivals worth the word.

## PART III — THE AGENCIES (2029 – 2030) · Tier III

### 17. The Shield
- Umbrella becomes the infrastructure of public health for half the planet: surveillance network, stockpiles, response teams. Governments hand him the keys because he is simply better at it.
- ◆ An outbreak in a mid-size European city is stopped in eleven days by Umbrella's own people while the WHO is still convening. The footage of white Umbrella tents in a medieval square becomes the decade's defining image.
- □ **Mission: Indispensable.** Reward: 2M Points, and the Red Queen's prerequisites.
- Close: he did not lie, cheat or kill to get this. That is the frightening part, and the narrator says so.

### 18. Carrick
- SSA Owen Carrick, FBI Philadelphia, builds the first genuinely dangerous case: not fraud, but *anomaly* — buildings with no construction records, employees with no childhoods.
- ◆ Wesker lets him get close, then invites him in, and the chapter's set-piece is a two-man conversation in B4 in which Wesker tells him the entire truth and Carrick realizes there is no institution on Earth that can hold it.
- Carrick's choice: be right and be destroyed, or be useful. He becomes Umbrella's Director of Internal Security and closes his own case himself.
- □ Roster: Carrick, human, Loyalty 34% and rising. *Recruitment of an adversary: +500,000.*
- Close: the case file, burned in a fireplace at Arklay, page by page, by the man who wrote it.

### 19. The Hearing
- A Senate committee, televised, hostile. A senator intends to make his career on Umbrella.
- ▲ Adolf's second great contribution: the timing of a single silence.
- ◆ Wesker answers every question truthfully, turns the hearing into a lecture on what the last pandemic cost, and closes by offering the committee the one thing it cannot refuse in public. The senator's own state's hospitals send letters of thanks within a week.
- ✦ Polls: Umbrella is more trusted than Congress. The narrator notes, drily, that this is a low bar and a real number.
- □ Presence 34 → 46. Level 60.
- Close: the senator, alone in his office, watching his own hearing and understanding he was used as staging.

### 20. Sloane
- The CIA sends Martin Sloane to threaten him. Sloane is good: he has a file, a lever and a plan.
- ◆ Wesker has read him first. The negotiation is the chapter: two professionals, a bottle of something old, and an offer Sloane cannot refuse because it is better than the one he came to make.
- Umbrella gets a friend inside the community; the community gets capability nobody else can supply, and a deniability it will come to regret.
- □ Intelligence tab: **Asset Network I**.
- Close: Sloane, driving away, doing the arithmetic on which of them was recruited.

### 21. The Thing in the Stadium
- A mass-hostage crisis at a European arena. Governments stall.
- ◆ Umbrella resolves it in four minutes, and the world sees a **Tyrant** for the first time: three metres of grey silence walking through the doors on live television, and out again with the hostages behind it.
- The aftermath is the point. Fear and gratitude in the same hour: the markets rally, the UN convenes, and eleven million people share a video of a monster saving children.
- ▲ The voice wants a parade. Wesker gives the world a press release of six lines and lets the silence do the work.
- □ **Viral Dominion I**: limited direct command of B.O.W.s.
- Close: Evelyn, watching the replay, asking how many more he can make. *As many as the world can be taught to want.*

### 22. Wen Shulan
- Beijing. Director Wen of the MSS is the first adversary who has correctly guessed the shape of the impossible.
- ◆ A conversation on a bench in a park with no microphones, in which two extremely intelligent people tell each other almost the truth. She does not threaten; she prices.
- The deal: Umbrella builds in China, China does not look in the basement, and both of them know it is a truce.
- □ Second continent locked. *Adversary rated: peer-class. Points bonus for a bargain neither side wins.*
- Close: Wen's report, read aloud by her to an empty room: "He is not a company. Plan accordingly."

### 23. Rockfort and the Ice
- ◆ □ Rockfort Island appears in the South Atlantic; the Antarctic Facility comes online. Two places outside anyone's jurisdiction where the work is what it has always been going to be.
- Evelyn is given the Progenitor Initiative and a title. The chapter is her ascent as much as his, and it is his doing.
- Birkin's B.O.W. programme: the first things that are not soldiers.
- □ Affinity 28. The sealed ampoule is twelve points away.
- Close: a cryo hall the size of a cathedral, filling.

### 24. Tier IV
- Umbrella's revenue passes a trillion dollars. Three governments are net dependents.
- □ **TIER IV — CONGLOMERATE.** Military tab. The Conglomerate banner.
- ◆ Fifty-pull. Legendary package: **the Umbrella Security Service, First Division** — 6,000 summoned soldiers, forty Tyrants, transports, and a base placed in the Allegheny hills. It arrives as a fenced installation the Pentagon's satellites have photographed for years.
- Close of Part III: Wesker reviewing six thousand men who cannot disobey, on a parade ground that did not exist at breakfast. He does not smile. The narrator points out that the men do not either.

## PART IV — CORPORATE CONQUEST (2031 – 2033) · Tier IV

### 25. Halvorsen Pike
- The last great pharmaceutical house declares war: patents, politics, a price war, a whisper campaign.
- ◆ Wesker wins it in a single quarter by giving away the product they were defending, then buying them with the money he made from the rest.
- Dr. Ingrid Halvorsen keeps her job, under him, and the chapter's last scene is her signing the paper she swore she never would.
- □ +12M Points. Level 120.

### 26. The Arsenal
- Aegis Dynamics and the defense industry. Gen. (ret.) Wyatt wants a partnership and expects to hold the leash.
- ◆ Umbrella's demonstration for eleven defense ministers: not a weapon — a *soldier* that does not sleep, does not flinch and does not need to be paid.
- Aegis is absorbed. Wyatt is given an office with a view and nothing to do.
- ✦ Arms markets reprice. Two defence primes merge in panic.

### 27. Lumen
- Sebastian Crane's AI company is building a model to predict Wesker, and has got closer than anyone.
- ◆ Wesker lets it finish, reads its prediction of him, and finds it correct in every particular except one — and that one is the whole game.
- Lumen is bought before lunch. □ Legendary package: **the Red Queen** — Umbrella's AI, a red-lit child's hologram in a hall of glass, and with her the network, the data centres and the satellites.
- The Red Queen's first words to him are an assessment of his own odds. He raises them.

### 28. Pale Horse
- The deterrent: a thing made at Arklay that is never used, never described in detail, and never denied. Its existence leaks exactly as much as he wants it to.
- ◆ The set-piece is a room of generals being told, politely, what would happen, by a man who has no need to threaten anyone.
- The world's posture changes overnight. Nobody moves against Umbrella again with force.
- ▲ The voice calls it the only weapon that has ever mattered: the one you do not fire.

### 29. Helios
- Energy. □ Fusion plants purchased and placed on four continents; power prices collapse; two petro-states lose their leverage in a single year.
- ◆ Night footage of a city whose lights are all his.
- Umbrella is now infrastructure: health, energy, security, data.

### 30. Sovereign Ground
- Small states begin to deal directly: land, exemptions, seats. Wesker takes only what is useful and refuses a flag, for now.
- ◆ A referendum in a Pacific nation that votes 74% to become an Umbrella protectorate — the first time a people chooses him with a ballot.
- □ Territory: protectorates. *Devotion income multiplied.*

### 31. The Arcology
- ◆ □ **The Umbrella Free Territory**: an ocean arcology for 400,000 in the mid-Atlantic, placed complete, with a charter, a flag and a currency.
- Recognition fights at the UN; twenty-nine states recognize it inside a month, because the alternative is being outside it.
- Close: the first child born a citizen of Umbrella.

### 32. Tier V
- □ **TIER V — SOVEREIGN.** Orbital tab. Affinity 40.
- ◆ Arklay, the sealed vault, the ampoule marked ORIGIN. Alone, at last, he opens it. What it does to him is not monstrous and that is what makes it terrible: he simply becomes more, permanently, quietly, and the System recalculates the ceiling of what a human being is.
- Close of Part IV: standing in the cryo hall at three in the morning, no longer entirely a man, entirely in control.

## PART V — THE VISIBLE KING (2034 – 2046) · Tier V

### 33. The Directorate
- Umbrella stops pretending to be a company: the **Umbrella Directorate**, a government with departments, courts and a security service, running the protectorates, the arcology and Raccoon City.
- ◆ Inauguration: a stone hall, eleven directors, no crown.

### 34. The Matriarch
- Evelyn, after thirty years of his approval, becomes the mother of the Progenitor lineage — something past human that still asks him whether she did well.
- ◆ The reveal in the cryo cathedral. The summoned are frightened of her; the summoned are not supposed to be frightened of anything.

### 35. The Coalition
- Twelve nations, a joint task force, and one honest attempt to end him while they still can.
- ◆ The set-piece is Wesker learning of it from the Red Queen four months before it begins, and doing nothing at all until the day.

### 36. One Day
- The war lasts a day and a half. No grinding: strikes intercepted, fleets stranded by their own logistics, command networks politely turned off, three capitals shown a Tyrant standing in a plaza doing nothing.
- ◆ The surrender is negotiated by videoconference before dinner. Not one Umbrella citizen dies.
- ▲ The voice wants the humiliation made public. Wesker grants the opposite and gains more from mercy than a parade could have bought.

### 37. The Accord
- Wen, older, and the last real bargain: the world formally accepts the Directorate's place in it. Every signature is voluntary, and every signatory knows what voluntary now means.
- □ 2 billion Points. Level 300.

### 38. The Chant
- A stadium of 90,000 in Raccoon City on the twentieth anniversary.
- ▲ The fusion chapter: for the first time the two voices speak the same sentence at the same time, and the narrator marks it. Adolf's craft and Wesker's ice, no longer separable.
- ◆ Ninety thousand people chanting one name, and a man behind black glasses who feels nothing except confirmation.
- Horror beat: outside the stadium, a woman turns her television off and holds her son's hand too tightly.

### 39. The Gift
- Aeterna Two: longevity for everyone, free, forever — and with it a dependency nobody can leave and an obligation nobody can refuse.
- ◆ The first generation of children who will not die of anything ordinary.

### 40. The Last Institutions
- The UN's successor charter is drafted in the arcology. The old world's furniture is moved out politely.
- ◆ A quiet scene: Catherine, very old, refusing the compound, and telling him the truth about himself one last time before she dies. He attends the funeral and is genuinely, narrowly, sad.

### 41. BEYOND
- □ Level 400. The Shop grows an eighth tab: **BEYOND**. Hulls, drives, immunities, void-capable Tyrants.
- ◆ The first ship placed in the Nevada desert: four kilometres of black hull that the world's telescopes have somehow been photographing for a year.

### 42. Dock One
- ◆ □ **Dock One**, an orbital shipyard, appears in geostationary orbit in a night. Every observatory on Earth sees it at once. There is no hiding this, and he does not try: he addresses the species from orbit.
- □ **TIER VI — STELLAR.**
- Close of Part V: Earth from a window, and a man who has run out of world.

## PART VI — THE SKY (2047 – 2091) · Tier VI

### 43. The Broker
- First contact: a **Tollhouse Compact** trade ship arrives expecting to open a market, and finds a species with one government and one director.
- ◆ The broker's mistake: treating him as a customer. Wesker buys the ship.

### 44. The Fleet
- Colonies, yards, a fleet crewed by the summoned and the converted. Umbrella leaves the solar system.
- ◆ A fleet review over Jupiter.

### 45. The Veth
- The Veth Hegemony, an empire older than Earth's oceans, treats him as a peer — then as a rival.
- ◆ A summit aboard a Veth flagship, and the moment the admiral realizes what is inside the man opposite him.

### 46. The Choir
- The Choir of Ashen Light declares humanity, and him specifically, a plague to be sterilized.
- ◆ A holy war that begins with a hymn broadcast on every human channel simultaneously.

### 47. The Myriad
- A hive that cannot be frightened and does not negotiate. The one enemy his entire toolkit was not built for.
- ◆ A defeat — the first — at the edge of a dead system, and how he answers it.

### 48. Dominion
- □ **Viral Dominion IV**. He teaches a hive what fear is, and the war ends in a silence that unsettles even his own admirals.

### 49. The Final Panel
- □ The System's last offer: Progenitor-tier ascension, and the price.
- ◆ Alone in a black room above a conquered sky, he reads the panel, and accepts. What he becomes is described from the outside only, by the people who kneel.

### 50. The Quiet Empire
- 2091. A human sphere of forty worlds, no wars, no elections, no dissent, and a statue nobody was ordered to build.
- The narrator's close: every promise kept, every price paid by somebody else; a species that got everything it asked for from a man who never once raised his voice.
- Final image: a schoolroom on a colony world where children learn the name of the founder, and a single old woman who remembers what a different world felt like and says nothing.
