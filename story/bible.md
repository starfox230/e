# UMBRELLA — Story Bible (rebuild, v2)

**Working title:** *What If Hitler Was Reborn as Albert Wesker… With a System to Build Umbrella?*
**Format:** ~12h dramatized YouTube What-If epic in 50 chapters and six Parts. Narrator, character voices, on-screen System panels, anime-style scene art, and a sound and music bed in the *Chrysalis* audiobook tradition.

---

## 1. Contract with the viewer

- **This is Wesker's story.** Every chapter is about him building Umbrella, gaining power, gaining loyal subordinates, or showing the world something it cannot explain. Side characters exist to react to him, serve him, or be swept aside by him.
- **Not a realistic scenario — a realistic Earth.** Real cities, real institutions, real money, real 2026 technology. The System breaks the rules of that Earth openly and often. We never grind through paperwork, landlords, payroll, inspections or permits. The System owns what it gives him, and what it gives him is his.
- **Forward, always.** Every chapter ends bigger than it began. A new facility, a new rank, a new capability, a new territory, a new kind of subordinate. No chapter is spent recovering, waiting or losing.
- **Shock and awe.** Each chapter has at least one showpiece: something appearing, something waking, something bowing, a room falling silent, a market convulsing, a skyline changing overnight.
- **Few enemies, dispatched fast.** An obstacle lasts one chapter at most. Most adversaries end the chapter as his subordinates. Nobody out-thinks him.
- **Worldbuilding.** The world reacts: newsrooms, markets, governments, ordinary people who look at a new tower and decide it was always there. Short vignettes, never side stories.
- **The narrator** is a calm, awed documentarian. Not his fan and not his critic: he reports what happened and lets it be enormous.

### Guardrails (my own, non-negotiable)
- Adolf is the man history recorded: the dictator who started the Second World War and whose regime murdered millions. The narration states that once, plainly, and does not linger. He never voices, praises or reminisces about his old ideology, symbols, regime or crimes. He is never admired by the narration or by other characters. No Nazi symbols anywhere.
- His function in the story is narrow: **public performance** — speeches, stagecraft, timing, reading a crowd. His lines are about craft, never about belief.
- Real sitting leaders are never named or shown. Presidents and ministers are offstage or fictional. Real agencies appear as institutions; every person in them is fictional. Rival companies are fictional.
- Biology is fictional or general textbook science (cell biology, immunology, protein chemistry). Nothing that works as a real-world method for making anything dangerous.
- No franchise logo. Umbrella's mark in pictures is an original crimson-and-white six-blade iris.

## 2. The Man

**Albert Wesker.** Tall, pale, athletic. **Blond hair slicked straight back. Black sunglasses, always, indoors and out.** Long black coat, black high-collared shirt, black gloves. Behind the glasses: red-amber eyes with slit pupils that glow when he is displeased. Voice: low, cold, measured, faintly amused; never raised.

- He died in a volcano in Africa, in another world, in 2009, having tried to make himself a god. He remembers all of it: Umbrella, Spencer, Birkin, the viruses, the Tyrants, the failure. He does not intend to fail twice.
- He carries his old virus-enhanced body from the first second: superhuman speed (reads as teleportation), strength, reflexes, regeneration. He never has to prove he is dangerous; he chooses when to show it.
- **Personality:** cold, cunning, patient, manipulative, always in control. He plans five moves ahead and lets people walk into the fourth. He never explains himself to subordinates and never negotiates with them. He does not reward people with money or praise; being useful to him is the reward, and his rare approval is currency people starve for.
- **Aura:** he arrives last and speaks least. He removes the glasses only when a threat becomes personal. He knows names, debts and secrets before the meeting starts. He stands unharmed where he should be dead and asks whether the meeting may continue.
- **Science:** he is a genuine genius virologist and geneticist. Whenever science is on the table he says something specific and correct that nobody else in the room saw. As his Intelligence stat rises, his insight visibly sharpens (Part I: sharp; Part III: startling; Part V: beyond human; Part VI: alien).

### The other voice — Adolf
- Merged into the same body by the System. Wesker holds the body and the will, completely, from the first minute. Adolf is a voice inside him and nothing more.
- **What Adolf is for:** Wesker is magnificent in a boardroom and terrifying one-to-one, but cold in front of a crowd. Adolf is the opposite: useless in a lab, but he understands a crowd — pacing, pauses, when to be silent, where to stand, how a room breathes. When a public speech matters, Wesker lets Adolf shape it, and the result — Wesker's presence carried on Adolf's timing — is mesmerizing.
- Their relationship: Wesker tolerates him because he is useful, and tells him so. Adolf resents being a passenger but respects results. Dry, sharp exchanges, short. Adolf never gets his way on anything but the stage.
- In the script he speaks as **ADOLF:** (interior voice, older, rougher, a little reverberant). The narrator always cues his lines ("the other voice stirred", "from somewhere behind his eyes", …) so he never speaks out of nowhere. He speaks rarely: a few lines per chapter at most, none in some chapters.

## 3. The System

A private interface only Wesker (and the voice inside him) can perceive. It has no name. Panels of cold white glass with crimson edges. **One small, quiet ding when a panel opens.** Text types itself. It speaks in a flat, precise feminine voice (SYSTEM) and reads a short version of each panel.

### Features (explained in full in Chapter 1)
1. **STATUS** — name, title, level, stats, free stat points.
2. **POINTS** — the currency. Earned by: Umbrella's growth (assets, revenue, territory), completed missions, the devotion of his people, acting brilliantly, and subjugating rivals. *The System rewards will made real.*
3. **SHOP** — tabs: Personnel · Facilities · Vehicles · Blueprints · Viral · Abilities · Assets. Later: Territory (Part II), Intelligence (Part III), Military (Part IV), Orbital (Part V), BEYOND (Ch 41). Prices scale; tabs unlock with tier.
4. **GACHA** — banners. Rarity: **Common** (grey) · **Uncommon** (green) · **Rare** (blue) · **Epic** (violet) · **Legendary** (gold) · **Progenitor** (black with a crimson edge; from Part III). A ten-pull guarantees one Rare or better.
   - A single Tyrant is **Rare**. Epic is a named specialist, a unique B.O.W., a major facility or a strong ability. **Legendary is a package** — a facility with its staff, soldiers and Tyrants inside — or a massive ability or stat surge. Progenitor is something that changes what he is.
5. **MISSIONS** — Main (tier objectives), Side, Hidden. Each shows a reward.
6. **ROSTER** — every person he commands. Summoned personnel: *Loyalty: Absolute*. Humans: a Loyalty percentage he can watch rise.
7. **PLACEMENT and INTEGRATION** — anything he buys or pulls appears **where he decides**: a lab under the car park, a tower on a skyline, a jet in a hangar. Then the world integrates it: deeds, permits, payroll, history and memory adjust. People who cannot perceive the System either remember it being built, believe it was always there, or simply do not notice until they look straight at it — and then shrug. Summoned people arrive with complete lives on paper. Only Wesker sees the seam. Proof never exists; the paperwork is always perfect.
8. **TIERS** — Umbrella's rank in the System, and the six Parts of the story:
   | Tier | Name | Reached |
   |---|---|---|
   | I | Garage | start (Ch 1) |
   | II | Company | end of Ch 8 |
   | III | Agency | end of Ch 16 |
   | IV | Conglomerate | end of Ch 24 |
   | V | Sovereign | end of Ch 32 |
   | VI | Stellar | end of Ch 42 |
   | — | Final | Ch 49 |
   The System calls his starting tier **Garage**. He takes that personally.

## 5. Places

- **Umbrella Headquarters, Wilmington, Delaware** — a six-storey white stone-and-glass building on the Christina riverfront with a crimson iris over the door and a three-level underground garage. Appeared on 14 September 2026. The whole city believes it has stood there since 1998. Owned outright by Umbrella.
- **Sublevel B4** — the first purchased facility: a laboratory level beneath the garage (Ch 2).
- **Umbrella Campus** — five buildings on the riverfront around HQ (Ch 6).
- **Newark Plant** — pharmaceutical production plant (Ch 4).
- **The Arklay Estate** — a mansion in the Allegheny forest of western Pennsylvania with a vast laboratory complex beneath it (Ch 8).
- **Raccoon City** — a company city of 100,000 in the Allegheny valley below Arklay (Ch 10). Later Umbrella's capital.
- **Rockfort Island** (South Atlantic) and **the Antarctic Facility** (Ch 23).
- **Umbrella Towers** in London, Singapore, Zurich, Tokyo (Ch 12–13).
- **Helios** fusion plants (Ch 29). **The Arcology** — the Umbrella Free Territory in the Atlantic (Ch 31).
- **Dock One** — the orbital shipyard (Ch 42).

## 7. Audio and picture language

- **Music:** dark orchestral. Cold piano and low strings for Wesker alone; pulse and synth for markets and schemes; brass and choir for speeches and reveals; triumph for tier-ups; horror only for B.O.W.s and Tyrants; wide cold pads for space. **Music follows the scene's mood: never suspense under a calm scene.**
- **Sound:** sparse, clean, realistic: footsteps on stone, doors, elevators, rain, city, crowds, helicopters, jets, gunfire, explosions (recorded, not synthesized). One small quiet ding for the System.
- **Images:** anime / cel-shaded illustration, cinematic framing. Crimson, white and black for Umbrella; cool blue city nights; gold light for Legendary pulls. Wesker always in black with sunglasses.
- **System panels:** on-screen glass windows with typed text; the ding fires first, the SYSTEM voice follows.
